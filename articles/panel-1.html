Cheryl built the company because one kid got sick and one did not

Two kids, born two different ways.

Her daughter came by C-section and ended up with eczema, allergies, food sensitivities. Her son came vaginally and had none of it.

Same parents. Same house. Completely different microbiomes from day one.

So she went looking for the science and found it sitting right there. C-section babies carry four times the risk of obesity, asthma and allergies, because they miss the microbial handoff in the birth canal.

The research existed. The product for parents did not. She built it.

Tiny Health now has 125,000 gut samples sequenced. Largest microbiome dataset for babies and toddlers in the world!

When gut health gets its mainstream moment the way sleep got one with Eight Sleep and Whoop, Cheryl already owns the data nobody else has.

Scott's whole thesis is one number

Scott put a number on the table that stopped the room. 50 million Americans have chronic digestive disease. One in seven people.

These are not people who are occasionally uncomfortable. They are managing real daily pain and getting close to nothing from the medical system.

His bet: gut health today sits exactly where sleep sat five years ago. Everybody knew sleep mattered. The products that made it measurable had not landed yet.

Now they are everywhere.

Gut health has that same energy right now. Awareness is climbing, products are coming, and the brands already inside the category with the data and the trust take the next five years.

"We are at a gut health revolution moment right now. This is the same place that sleep was 3-5 years ago."

Scott Hinkle, Co-founder, Throne Science

Function's physician problem, and how they got around it

Pranitha's founding challenge was pricing 160 biomarkers so a normal person could afford them, not just executives and pro athletes.

That breaks how labs have worked for decades.

Physicians pushed back hard. "Our results are scary" was the line coming back from the medical establishment.

Function's answer was to publish the methodology, partner with doctors instead of routing around them, and let the data argue over time. Results ship with context and guidance, never raw numbers dumped on somebody with no support.

And the flywheel is turning. Members bring results to their doctor, the doctor sees 160 biomarkers they did not know to order, and some of those doctors turn into Function's loudest advocates.

Cheryl reads 10,000 customer emails herself

This one detail tells you how she runs the company.

Not summaries. Actual emails, at volume, for years.

For years.

What she found in them: customers were ordering as a last resort, after multiple doctors gave them nothing. Desperate parents with sick kids who found Tiny Health once the system had failed them.

That changed how she talks about the product, who the advocates are, and what gets built next.

You do not get that from a dashboard. You get it by reading the emails.

"The parents that are the biggest advocates for us are the ones that got their kid healthy."

Cheryl Sihoi, Co-founder, Tiny Health

AI is changing the patient faster than it is changing the doctor

All three agreed on this one.

Patients are decoding their own test results before the appointment. Researching diagnoses, asking follow ups, finding specialists their GP never mentioned.

The information gap that defined the doctor relationship is closing fast.

For brands like these, that is a serious tailwind. If a parent asks ChatGPT how to understand their kid's gut health, the answer points at exactly the kind of detailed, data-backed content these companies have been publishing for years.

The brands that built on education and trust are the ones the AI serves.

17 years for research to reach practice does not hold when patients can read the research tonight. Something breaks. These are the founders building for after it does.

What to take home

  • Cheryl's email habit is a repeatable strategy, not a quirk. Founders who stay in the raw customer language, not the summary of it, build the right thing more often.
  • Function's playbook when you disrupt the establishment: bring the doctors into the data instead of going around them. Critics become advocates.
  • Scott's timing framework travels well past gut health. Find where consumer awareness is outrunning the product infrastructure. That gap is the business.
  • The 17 year research-to-practice lag is structural. Brands bridging it are serving people the system decided to make wait.
  • If AI surfaces you when someone asks about your category, that is earned media no ad budget buys. Build the content and the community it trusts.
