The bomb squad experiment

Whoop put devices on bomb disposal units with the Department of Defense.

The question was whether wearable data could improve decisions in a place where a bad call kills people.

John Sullivan says the answer changed how he thinks about data entirely, He was fired up telling it.

Operators could see exactly how their physiology moved in specific situations. Not abstract trends. Their own strain, their own recovery, their own stress markers pinned to real moments in the field.

That is the use case Whoop actually cares about. A mirror, not a scoreboard.

"We're in the business of behavior change," he said. When the stakes are real and the feedback is specific, people change how they operate.

The campaign that worked too well

Whoop launched "Be Obsessed" to celebrate people pushing their limits. It hit the target audience clean.

Then it pulled in a crowd they never wanted. People bragging about terrible sleep scores. Ten day streaks of red recovery worn as a flex.

The campaign built hype and imported users who had the product completely backwards.

Those users churned. All of them.

Brand energy is easy to generate. Brand meaning is the hard part to protect.

The community your loudest marketing attracts is often not the community that keeps you alive.

"We always want to attract the right member rather than the wrong member. We would rather slow down our marketing approach than attract the wrong member at the wrong time."

John Sullivan, CMO, Whoop (9 years)

The HealthSpan screenshot broke their record in a week

Whoop shipped a HealthSpan metric. Inside one week it became the most shared screenshot in the history of the app.

Members had been using recovery and strain to optimize today. HealthSpan showed them the cumulative bill. Years of healthy life you are adding or subtracting, instead of how recovered you are on a Tuesday.

That landed differently, and people shared it because it said something real about them.

Then John got personal. He found out his APOB cholesterol was five times the outer limit.

No symptoms. No warning. A number on a screen that changed what he does every day.

Higher Dose pivoted on a product they almost said no to

Ingrid Millman-Cordy and her co-founder were running a biohacking spa in New York. They wanted infrared sauna and could not afford the buildout.

A vendor showed up with a portable sauna blanket. They said yes, expecting a small side product.

It became the entire business!

The blanket took the sauna out of the facility and put it in people's homes. Suddenly it worked for anyone who did not live near a location, could not spend $50 a session, and wanted it on their own schedule.

They are on the fourth generation now, and a 30% repeat purchase rate tells you the community around it is real.

"The data is a weather forecast, not a report card. Use it to plan, not to grade yourself."

John Sullivan, CMO, Whoop

Weather forecast, not report card

This is the framing John uses now, inside the company and out.

A report card judges what already happened. A forecast is a tool for what you do next.

So when a member opens their recovery score, the question should be what am I doing today.

That reframe decides how long they stay. Members who treat it as a forecast stick. The ones grading themselves burn out and churn.

Higher Dose gets there through a different door. Their products are built for a daily ritual that compounds. "You feel better, you look better, and you want to do it again." The loop is the product.

Ingrid on the community she has and the one she wants

Her most honest moment on stage. Higher Dose skews heavily female, because that is who found them first and who they built around.

Now she is working out how to grow the male side without breaking the trust that got her here.

"We're being very intentional about not alienating the audience that got us here while also growing."

John's version: Whoop's community is global and evangelical, and the evangelism has to sit on real behavior change. One member who actually changes their training and sleep is worth ten wearing it as a status signal. Retention is the receipt.

What to take home

  • The bomb squad study is the data use case to copy. High stakes, specific feedback, real behavior change. If your data can do that for a customer you have a business. If it is decorative you have a gadget.
  • The "Be Obsessed" lesson is worth a meeting. Know who you are actually building with before you pick your loudest message.
  • Ingrid's sauna blanket is a category creation story that started as a cost problem in her own spa. Solve your own constraint and check whether it is somebody else's product.
  • Weather forecast framing travels to any data product. If customers are grading themselves with your numbers, they churn. Reframe it before it ships.
  • John's APOB story closes the pitch every time. Find the equivalent in your product, the invisible thing your data caught, and tell it.
